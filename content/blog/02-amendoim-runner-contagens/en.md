Brazil has become one of the three largest peanut exporters in the world, and almost all of it comes from one place: the interior of São Paulo state, where the crop is planted in October and November and harvested from February to May. The variety is Runner — a medium-sized, uniform kernel with a red skin — and it is graded in a way that is simple once you know the rule.

## Count per ounce

Shelled peanuts are sold by **count**: the number of kernels in one ounce (28.35 g). A count of **38/42** means that between 38 and 42 kernels make an ounce — the largest kernels of the crop. From there the counts step down: 40/50, 50/60, 60/70, 70/80 and **80/100**, the smallest whole kernel. The lower the numbers, the bigger the kernel and, usually, the higher the price.

Which count you need depends on the use. Snack and cocktail-nut roasting call for large, uniform kernels — 38/42 or 40/50. Peanut butter, confectionery pastes and oil do not care about size and are happier with 60/70, 70/80 or **splits**, the halves that separate naturally during shelling and blanching. Splits are cheaper per tonne and identical in flavour.

## Raw or blanched

**Raw** kernels keep the red skin. That is the form for oil pressing, for markets that roast with the skin on and for buyers who blanch at destination. **Blanched** kernels have the skin removed by heat and friction and are electronically sorted a second time; they are pale, uniform and ready for snack lines, confectionery and peanut butter. Blanched kernels are usually offered in 38/42 and in splits, and can be roasted to order.

## The aflatoxin question

Every serious peanut conversation reaches aflatoxin. It is a natural toxin from a mould that can develop in the field or in storage, and every importing market sets a limit for it. The European Union is the strictest: 2 µg/kg of aflatoxin B1 and 4 µg/kg total for peanuts going straight to consumption, and 8 and 15 for peanuts to be sorted or processed before eating. Other markets allow more.

What matters for you is that the limit is a specification, not a hope. Tell us the destination, and the lot is sorted and tested against that limit before it is bought, with the laboratory certificate travelling in the shipping documents. Electronic colour sorting removes the discoloured kernels that carry most of the risk; sampling follows the destination's method, since the EU in particular prescribes how a peanut lot must be sampled.

## Packing and container

Kernels ship in 25 kg or 50 kg bags — polypropylene or multiwall paper — or in 1,000 kg big bags for industrial buyers. A 20′ container takes about 18 to 19 tonnes in bags, a 40′ high cube about 25 to 26, the exact figure depending on the bag and whether the cargo is palletised. Peanuts are a living product: they travel best in a ventilated or, on long routes, a reefer container set to a mild temperature, and they should not sit in a hot port for weeks.

## Writing the specification

A complete peanut specification fits in one line: *Runner, blanched, 38/42, moisture max. 7 %, aflatoxin to EU limit, 25 kg bags, 1 × 40′ HC, CFR Rotterdam, shipment March.* With that, we can pull the right lot from the processors we work with, prepare a sample, and quote with the freight already in the number.
